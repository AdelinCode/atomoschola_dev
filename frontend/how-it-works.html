How Atomo Schola Works

Everything you need to know about the platform — content structure, roles, and how to become a contributor.

Overview Lesson Structure Content Types Roles Become an Editor Become a Creator Review Process Leaderboards Features

Atomo Schola is a community-driven educational platform organized around two major categories: STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering & Mathematics) and Humanities (History, Literature, Philosophy, Languages & Social Sciences).

Content is structured hierarchically — each category contains subjects, subjects contain domains, and domains contain lessons. Anyone can browse and read lessons freely; contributing requires an account and a specific role.

Lessons come in three formats:

  • Text Lessons Rich formatted content with LaTeX formula support
  • Video Lessons Embedded YouTube videos with descriptions
  • Presentations Interactive slide-based content

Every lesson must follow a standard structure. While most content is left to the creator's discretion, these five sections are required:

  1. 1
    Prerequisites List the knowledge assumed by the reader. Prefer linking to lessons within Atomo Schola; if unavailable, specify the topic with enough detail to avoid confusion. Total enumeration is not required — if calculus requires algebra, only algebra needs to be listed.
  2. 2
    The Lesson The core content, in any supported format. Should be understandable without a teacher — thorough explanations and examples required. Focus on understanding, not memorization; material that must be memorized belongs in the Summary.
  3. 3
    Summary A concise summary of everything that must be memorized — formulas, theorems, key phenomena. Students should be able to review using only the summary.
  4. 4
    Exercises Practice problems covering a range of difficulties. Enough for thorough practice at the creator's discretion.
  5. 5
    Bibliography Sources used to create the lesson, sufficient to verify accuracy. Original explanations not found in sources should be clearly marked as such.

Roles are hierarchical — each role includes all permissions of the roles below it. Creators are also editors and users; editors are also users.

User Browse, bookmark and rate lessons
Editor Everything a User can do, plus vote on proposed lessons
Creator Everything an Editor can do, plus create and publish lessons
Staff Moderate content and manage the platform

Editors join review panels and vote on lessons submitted by creators. To earn the Editor role, propose 3 edits to 3 different published lessons via the Propose Edits page. Each edit is reviewed by the Editor Commission.

  1. 1
    Propose edits to 3 different published lessons.
  2. 2
    Each edit is reviewed by the Editor Commission, which votes to accept or reject it.
  3. 3
    If all 3 edits are accepted, you officially become an Editor.
  4. 4
    As an Editor, you can join review panels and vote on new lesson submissions.

Creators write and publish lessons. To earn the Creator role you must first be an Editor, then submit a complete original lesson for review. The Council of Creators votes — a majority approval publishes the lesson and promotes you.

  1. 1
    Create a free account and become an Editor first.
  2. 2
    Prepare and submit a complete original lesson for the Council of Creators to review.
  3. 3
    The Council of Creators votes on your submission. A majority is required for approval.
  4. 4
    If approved, your lesson is published automatically and you receive the Creator role.

When a creator submits a lesson it appears on the Review Dashboard, visible to all editors and creators. The process is collaborative and panel-based.

  1. 1
    SubmissionThe lesson enters the Review Dashboard with status Under Review. Creators are limited to 1 submission per day.
  2. 2
    Panel AssemblyAny editor or creator (other than the lesson's author) can join the review panel. Up to 9 reviewers. Discussion happens on the platform's Discord in a private channel.
  3. 3
    Vote SessionWhen ready, the creator opens a vote session. All panel members are notified and cast YES or NO.
  4. 4
    Outcome7 YES votes → lesson published automatically. If all panel members vote without reaching 7 YES, the session closes as rejected — but the creator can open a new session after further improvements.
7 YES → published immediately < 7 YES → session rejected, can retry Staff can approve or reject directly Creator cannot join their own panel

Creators and editors each have their own leaderboard. Since creators also have editor privileges, a creator can appear on both simultaneously.

  • Creator leaderboard — requires at least 5 published lessons to qualify
  • Editor leaderboard — requires having voted on at least 15 accepted lessons
  • Score formula — 15 × (avg rating 0–3 months) + 10 × (avg rating 3–12 months) + 7.5 × (avg rating 12+ months) + 1 × (total published)
  • The top 7 creators and top 7 editors are invited to their respective commission for a one-month term
  • If someone declines the invitation, the spot passes to the next person on the leaderboard

All registered users have access to these features:

  • Advanced search across all subjects, domains and lessons
  • Bookmark your favourite lessons to find them quickly later
  • Rate and review lessons to help other learners
  • Rich text editor with LaTeX formula support for creators
  • Attach files from cloud storage to lessons
  • Inbox notifications for updates on proposals and edits
  • Use invite codes to upgrade your account role
